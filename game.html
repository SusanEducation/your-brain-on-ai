The science behind the game

Every scenario in this game is grounded in decades of research on how humans learn. The core insight: mental effort is not a bug in learning — it's the mechanism. The game uses three categories: AI habits that genuinely build skill (💪), habits that are largely neutral (😐), and habits that quietly steal your chance to develop (🚫).

🔁 Retrieval Practice (Testing Effect)

Retrieving information from memory strengthens the memory trace far more than re-reading the same material. Students who test themselves remember up to 50% more after a week than students who restudy. This is why generating your own answers — even wrong ones — matters so much.

😤 Desirable Difficulties

Certain kinds of difficulty during learning — struggling to recall, working through problems without hints, spacing out practice — lead to better long-term retention even though they feel harder in the moment. Easy is not the same as learned.

⚡ The Generation Effect

Generating an answer yourself before seeing the right one creates a significantly stronger memory than reading the correct answer directly. Your brain has to work harder to update a wrong answer — and that effort is precisely what builds the memory.

🏋️ Deliberate Practice and Neural Wiring

Practising skills with focus and effort literally changes the brain's physical structure — myelinating neural pathways and strengthening synaptic connections. Outsourcing the practice to AI means the wiring never forms.

📅 Spaced Practice

Reviewing material spread over time beats cramming. The gap between sessions — the forgetting and re-learning it requires — is what builds durable long-term memory.

🏳️ Learned Helplessness

When effort repeatedly feels unnecessary because help always arrives immediately, people gradually lose the belief that their effort makes a difference — and stop trying. AI that removes all struggle on demand can accelerate this pattern.
